Where the burndown leaks, and the five changes that stop it

Why the open-issue count stopped falling, which fixes actually ended their class, and where a structural change would replace a stream of instance fixes.

The backlog is not a plateau, and the fixes mostly are addressing roots. The open count was built by a four-week filing surge from three audits and has been draining since. Half of everything filed since late July is about the verification apparatus, not the library.

The recurring root sits one level up. Where a defect class got a type (wire-width init { require } plus its TCK), the class ended. Where it got a lexical guard, a paragraph, or a TCK obligation for a property only a type can carry, the remedy's own blind spot became the next issue. That pattern accounts for 47 issues since week 31 and the whole cancellation and roster families.

The lever is to stop paying for guards where a type would do. Five structural changes below retire two thirds of the guard file, the two largest issue families, and most of the exception-discipline prose.

03Five structural levers

Ranked by recurrence removed, not by size. Each one names the first PR and what it retires. Four of the five are corrections of scope rather than new machinery: the primitive already exists and was left optional.

Lever 1 · Carry the contract obligations in types, not in prose

The Seam contract is 10 members and 28 "must/never" sentences, implemented 34 times. Five obligations are re-implemented per seam and each has a guard or a paragraph policing it. In five cases the un-bypassable form was a small edit away.

  • Seam.state: GatedStateFlow with a non-public constructor. A bare MutableStateFlow<SeamState> can then no longer satisfy the interface. Today forbidBareSeamStateFlow fires on nothing: all 9 production sites carry an ALLOW marker.
  • PeerRoster in :kuilt-core owning collapse-then-latch atomically with selfId non-removable, the way SeamStateGate owns the state write. Five mechanisms exist for this one invariant today; four sites write .value= unguarded. #2654 already argues for it.
  • Seam.trySendTo / tryBroadcast returning a sealed SendOutcome. 76 of 368 catch sites guard a best-effort send; 61 of those only log or discard. broadcast already made this choice in its KDoc. Two default methods, no fabric changes.
  • closeAll(items) / closeQuietly(), the loop discipline written once. Removes 55 sites and the four sites the audit found wrong by CLAUDE.md's own rules (NwSeam.kt:2244, NwBridgeRuntime.kt:263, NwBridgeExports.kt:76, RaftEngine.kt:3819).
  • Non-inline withLockNoSuspend. A suspend call in the body becomes a compile error, replacing a 215-line scanner that admits it cannot tell Seam.close() from Channel.close().
  • kuiltRunTest { } in :kuilt-test with no timeout parameter, replacing the 801-line ceiling guard.
Retires
  • ~200 catch sites (52% of 368)
  • 6 guards, ~2,450 lines
  • the 132-line exception section of CLAUDE.md (14% of the file)
  • the #1816 family, 35 issues so far

Lever 2 · Route the five radios through the skeleton that already exists

kuilt already has the shared seam skeleton, by composition: a fabric implements Connection (four members) and calls identified() or peerMesh(), which return a LinkSeam or MeshSeam owning gate, spool, pumps, roster, budget and the self-send guard. WebSocket, TCP and mDNS take that path. NwSeam, NearbySeam, MCSessionLink, BridgePeerLink and WebRTCPeerLink did not, and re-derived MeshSeam by hand: ~610 lines of load-bearing duplication, one byte-identical copy of a security-relevant function (canonicalLinkNonce), and 20 verbatim copies of the self-send require.

The one recorded objection (the 2026-07-10 lifecycle design, Option B) rejects an inherited teardown protocol, and its own residual-seam note parks the roster half "not justified by five call sites". That count is twelve now. Composition through Connection keeps each seam's teardown, so the objection does not apply.

Order: WebRTCPeerLink (smallest, its own KDoc points at the gate), then the two multipeer links together (near-copies), then Nearby, then Nw last because its drain window is richer than MeshSeam's and may need to move up rather than down.

Retires
  • ~1,085 of 3,249 seam code lines
  • 4 guards that exist only for this
  • 5 of the 9 ALLOW-bareSeamState markers
  • the decorator budget divergence (#2642, a live bug)

Lever 3 · One anti-vacuity mechanism, named, and mandatory

Five different mechanisms exist for "a suite must be able to fail": sealed two-armed fixtures (Bolt, DurableStore, Discovery, Room), ObligationDeclaration cross-checked against a hook (Seam, WireCodec), declared vacuity floors with a self-test (Lattice), a strength enum with both arms failing (CommutativeScheme), and an adversarial corpus (Wasm). Nothing in :kuilt-conformance names the concept, so each new suite author picks one by luck, and three suites are still reference-only (Mesh, CloseableLifecycle, RaftStorage, whose own KDoc says it discriminates nothing). QuiltedConformanceSuite keeps a nullable opt-out across 24 subclasses.

The fault injector also already exists and is public: FaultyLoom in :kuilt-test is strictly stronger than the internal ChaosSeam on every axis but duplicate. Exactly one of 17 suites requires it. SeamConformanceSuite, with 38 harnesses, never imports it, so no fabric in kuilt is conformance-tested under drop, delay or reorder. #1819 (16 bytes leave a Nearby seam permanently deaf) is the cost.

  • Generalise ObligationDeclaration out of the Seam suite; every published suite declares each unreachable failure as Gap or InapplicableByDesign, never null.
  • Add a FaultInjection fixture to SeamConformanceSuite in RoomConformanceSuite's shape; fold duplicate-delivery into FaultProfile; delete ChaosSeam.
  • Rule: every suite ships an executable rig test that runs it against a broken fixture and asserts red. Twelve already do; the prose-table alternative in RaftStorage is what rots.
  • Assert obligations on both roles. Sixteen of the Seam suite's obligations are host-only on a symmetric contract (#2591, #2601, #2697).
Retires
  • the #2247 audit as a recurring activity
  • the "fixture cannot fail" class (125 filed since W31)
  • ~1,000 lines of duplicated per-type law tests

Lever 4 · Move the guards, test them, and change the minting rule

The root build file is 8,246 lines, of which 64 are build configuration. It grew ~340 lines a day in the week before this audit, across 65 commits in 60 days, and no guard can be run or debugged in isolation because build-logic/ has no test source set. Four guards already carry (name, source, expected) case tables that run on every check and are directly portable to JUnit.

Classifying the 30 guards by the strongest enforcement available: 10 are type-shaped, 9 need a type-resolving linter, 5 are process or tool config, 6 are genuinely lexical (citations, inventories, samples). The three cancellation guards together see 27 of 368 sites, about 7%. Two guards are exempted across their entire live population.

  1. Mechanical move of all 30 plus plumbing into build-logic/src/main/kotlin/guards/, one file per family, with the four case tables promoted to src/test. Zero behaviour change.
  2. Adopt detekt 2.x under dev.detekt. #2540 measured 1.23.8; the argument does not carry to 2.x, which pins this repo's Kotlin.
  3. Fold Dokka failOnWarning, a nightly for the two meta-premise guards, and a CI step for test-result parity.
  4. Before any new check-wired scan: classify it T/C/L/P and write why the stronger class was rejected. Every new guard ships a committed positive control.
Retires
  • 24 of 30 guards
  • 8,246 → ~1,400 lines
  • the ~0.6 follow-up issues each guard has cost
  • most of the merge-conflict surface on one file

Lever 5 · Reopen the decompositions where the fix class is a missing decision layer

Both decompositions closed on 2026-07-07 and both files have since regrown past them: RaftEngine 1,158 → 1,685 code lines (4,330 raw), SeamRoom 624 → 1,351 code lines (3,758 raw). Neither has an open tracker, and WarpNode, the third giant the cleanup spec says was filed, never got an issue at all. The regrowth is new concerns landing inside the class, and each file's dominant fix class is the same shape: a decision made inline with I/O.

  • RaftEngine: 24 of 53 fixes are "an inbound or restored value was not validated". The repo already named the remedy, RefusalGate?-returning pure validators, and applied it to four private functions. Make them internal (cluster tests become unit tests), extend the pattern to the remaining sites, and move the 8 log-throttling fields out into a diagnostics object.
  • SeamRoom: two near-identical per-recipient lane implementations whose KDoc cross-references the duplication (~550 → ~150 lines), then a RelayPlane with a pure resolveRecipients. Reuse the shared lock the resume machine already documents; do not mint a second.
  • NwSeam: the displacement drain and the wedge watchdog are already separate concurrency domains (~620 lines, watchdog read-only).

Not god objects despite raw size: EntitlementLedger (zero mutable fields, lattice surface), SeamConformanceSuite (stateless by design, with a recorded rationale and #2439 as the only split with backing), CompositeSeam and MeshSeam (411 and 500 code lines; the volume is proof-comment, and they have the highest defect density per code line in the set).

Retires
  • the validation fix class in Raft (≈45% of its fixes)
  • ~400 duplicated lines in SeamRoom
  • the need for a simulated cluster to test a validator

04Cleanup ledger

Smaller items that fell out of the audits. None prevents a recorded incident; each removes a place where the next duplication would land.

Code

  • Seam.requireNotSelf(peer) replaces 20 verbatim copies, 5 inside :kuilt-core.
  • Hoist canonicalLinkNonce and toHex; MeshSeam.kt:616 and NwHello.kt:114 are byte-identical.
  • Seam.filteredBy(peer) replaces five hand-written per-peer view classes.
  • PeerlessSeam.close is a no-op and its incoming never completes.
  • Extend the ISE guard's type set to RuntimeException and Exception: 8 more arms swallow cancellation the same way.
  • :kuilt-otel-tap still depends on the superseded :kuilt-multipeer in appleMain.
  • :kuilt-conformance is a hub with api deps on raft, session, store and crdt, so every module's test classpath pulls the stack.

Tests

  • schedulerClock is byte-identical in 10 files across 4 modules.
  • tick() in 16 sites hand-syncs a second clock to virtual time; drift is silent.
  • Six websocket tests hand-roll connectPair beside the shared helper.
  • 24 hand-written single-pair CRDT law functions duplicate what LatticeLawSuite covers with 32 seeds.
  • :kuilt-quilter has 242 real advanceUntilIdle() calls against a re-arming timer loop; no guard exists for it.
  • ConcurrencyStressHarness is jvmMain-only with one user while nine ALLOW-realDispatcher sites describe it.

Claims that are now false

  • CLAUDE.md says CompositeSeam/CompositeLoom confinement is "legacy being migrated". Zero limitedParallelism uses remain in either; the migration is done.
  • CLAUDE.md says "nine flows are exempt" from the bare-state guard; that is the whole population, not a residue.
  • RefusalGate.kt KDoc lists adoptLeaderForTerm with three pure validators; it is suspend and mutates.
  • The cleanup-epic spec says WarpNode's decomposition was filed. It was not.
  • ~24 open issues are already satisfied on main and can close on inspection.

05What to change about the process

  • Label audit output. Half the backlog is findings from sweeps, not regressions. An audit-finding label lets the burndown count separate library defects from verification debt, which is the number the plateau worry was really about.
  • Type before guard. The reflex after an incident is a scan plus a paragraph. Seventeen of thirty guards protect a property with a stronger tool available, and in five the type already existed in :kuilt-core. Make "why not a type?" the first question, and a positive control the price of admission.
  • Put the why on the primitive. CLAUDE.md is 13,200 words with 72 commits touching it; much of it is rationale for disciplines that should be KDoc on the primitive that enforces them, or an ADR. Only six ADRs exist; the design specs under docs/superpowers/specs/ are the de-facto ADRs and are not linked as such.
  • Reopen a decomposition when its file doubles. Closing the tracker removed the pressure; both giants regrew past their filing size within eight weeks.

06Suggested order

  1. Move the 30 guards into build-logic/ with the four case tables as tests. Zero behaviour change; unblocks every later guard edit.
  2. Seam.trySendTo / tryBroadcast as default methods. Two methods, one sealed type, 76 sites migrate at leisure.
  3. GatedStateFlow on the contract and the 9-marker sweep in the same PR.
  4. PeerRoster in :kuilt-core, then WebRTCPeerLink onto Connection as the first radio migration and the template for the other four.
  5. Mandatory FaultInjection on SeamConformanceSuite; delete ChaosSeam.
  6. Reopen #1121 and #1122 at today's sizes; file the WarpNode tracker; make the four Raft validators internal.
